Jollof Recipe

Jollof Rice is a legendary, one-pot West African dish celebrated for its vibrant reddish-orange hue, deeply savory depth, and signature hint of spice. Long-grain rice gently steams inside a rich, concentrated reduction of blended Roma tomatoes, sweet red bell peppers, and fiery scotch bonnets, seasoned beautifully with curry, thyme, and bay leaves. Every grain absorbs the fragrant, smoky essence of the stew, creating a comforting dish that serves as the undisputed centerpiece of any festive gathering, party, or family feast.

  • 3 cups Long-grain parboiled rice (or Sella basmati rice)
  • 5 large Roma tomatoes
  • 2 medium Red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 1 to 2 Scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo, adjust to taste)
  • 2 medium Red onions (1 for blending, 1 finely sliced)
  • 6 Tomato paste
  • ¼ cup Vegetable or sunflower oil
  • 2 cups Rich chicken or beef broth
  • 3 Dried bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp Curry powder
  • 1 tsp Dried thyme
  • 2 Bouillon cubes (crushed)
  • Salt to taste

Steps

  1. Prepare the Pepper Base: Blend the tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnets, and one onion together until completely smooth. Pour into a pot and boil down over medium heat until the liquid reduces by half and forms a thick paste.
  2. Fry the Aromatics & Tomato Paste: Heat the oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Sauté the sliced onions for about 3 to 5 minutes until soft. Add the tomato paste and fry for about 10 to 15 minutes, stirring constantly, until it darkens slightly and takes on a grainy texture to eliminate any sour taste.
  3. Build the Sauce: Pour in your reduced pepper mix and stir thoroughly. Season with the curry powder, thyme, crushed bouillon cubes, bay leaves, and salt. Cover and let the stew simmer for 10 minutes until the oil begins to separate and rise to the top.
  4. Wash and Coat the Rice: Rinse your rice grains repeatedly in cold water until the water runs completely clear to remove excess surface starch. Drain the rice well, add it to the pot, and stir gently until every grain is thoroughly coated in the rich tomato stew.
  5. Steam to Perfection: Pour in the meat broth and just enough hot water so that the liquid sits barely above the rice grains. Stir once, then seal the pot tightly by placing a layer of aluminum foil or parchment paper over the top before shutting the lid firmly.
  6. Low-Heat Simmer: Lower the heat to the absolute minimum and let the rice steam for 30 to 40 minutes. Do not disturb it early; the tight seal forces the steam to cook the rice evenly.
  7. Fluff and Serve: Once the grains are tender and the liquid is absorbed, remove the lid, discard the bay leaves, and gently fluff the rice with a fork. Serve warm alongside fried plantains (dodo), grilled chicken, or beef.
