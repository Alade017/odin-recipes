Semo with egusi recipe

Semo (Semolina) with Egusi Soup is a beloved staple pairing in Nigerian households, prized for its exceptional rich texture and deep comfort factor. Semo provides a firm, slightly nutty, and perfectly smooth swallow that acts as a great anchor for Egusi—a complex, savory soup thickened with ground melon seeds, richly layered with palm oil, local aromatics, and loaded with assorted meats and leafy greens.

Part 1: Fried egusi soup recipe

Ingredients

  • 2 cups Ground Egusi (melon seeds)
  • ⅓ cup Palm oil
  • 3 large Red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 2 Scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo)
  • 1 large Red onion (half chopped, half for blending)
  • 2 tbsp Ground crayfish
  • 1 to 2 tbsp Iru (fermented locust beans)
  • Assorted meats & seafood: Beef, shaki (tripe), kpomo (cow skin), and stockfish (pre-boiled)
  • 1 piece Smoked fish (catfish or panla, cleaned and deboned)
  • 1 cup Fresh bitter leaf (washed thoroughly to reduce bitterness) or Ugu (fluted pumpkin leaves)
  • 2 Bouillon cubes
  • Salt to taste

Steps

  1. Prep the Egusi Paste: In a bowl, mix the ground egusi seeds with a tiny bit of warm water or meat stock to form a thick, moldable paste. Set aside.
  2. Make the Pepper Base: Roughly blend the red bell peppers, scotch bonnets, and half of the onion together.
  3. Fry the Aromatics: Heat the palm oil in a pot over medium heat for 2 minutes. Add the chopped onions and iru. Sauté for about 2 minutes until your kitchen fills with that distinctive, fragrant local aroma.
  4. Fry the Egusi Chunks: Drop the thick egusi paste into the hot oil in small, bite-sized spoonfuls or chunks. Do not stir immediately. Let the chunks fry undisturbed on medium-low heat for about 5 to 7 minutes until they tighten up and form cooked, egg-like lumps. Gently flip them over to fry the other side.
  5. Add the Peppers & Base: Gently pour the blended pepper mix around the egusi lumps. Stir carefully so you do not break the lumps down entirely. Let it cook together for 10 minutes until the oil begins to rise to the top.
  6. Incorporate Stock and Meats: Pour in your pre-cooked assorted meats, stockfish, ground crayfish, crushed bouillon cubes, and a cup of your meat stock. Simmer for 10 minutes so all the proteins drink in the egusi flavors. Gently fold in the deboned smoked fish.
  7. Add the Greens: Throw in your washed bitter leaf or chopped Ugu leaves. Stir evenly and let the entire pot simmer on low heat for a final 3 to 5 minutes. Taste and adjust with salt if necessary, then turn off the flame.

Part 2: Semo(Semovita) recipe

Ingredients

  • 2 cups of semovita flour
  • Water

Steps

  1. Boil the Base: Bring 2½ cups of water to a rolling boil in a clean pot over high heat.
  2. Create the Slurry: In a small bowl, mix about 3 tablespoons of dry semolina flour with ½ cup of cold water until it forms a completely smooth, watery paste.
  3. Pour and Stir: Reduce the pot's heat to low. Pour the cold slurry directly into the boiling water while whisking continuously with a wooden spatula (omorogun). This creates a smooth, thickened pudding base that blocks lumps from forming later.
  4. Add the Dry Flour: Slowly rain the remaining dry semolina flour into the pot with one hand while vigorously turning and stirring the mixture with the spatula using your other hand.
  5. Gather and Knead: Keep stirring, pressing, and kneading the dough against the sides of the pot until it clumps together into a smooth, firm, uniform mass.
  6. Steam Through: If the semo feels too stiff, sprinkle 2 tablespoons of water around the edges of the pot. Cover the lid and let it steam on the lowest heat setting for 2 to 3 minutes to cook the grains completely.
  7. Final Fluff: Open the lid and give it one final vigorous knead until it becomes stretchy and glossy. Scoop it out, shape it into a smooth presentation ball, and serve steaming hot with your fresh Egusi Soup.
