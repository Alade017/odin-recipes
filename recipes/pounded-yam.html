Pounded yam with efo riri recipe

Pounded Yam is a revered, staple Nigerian swallow celebrated for its luxuriously smooth, stretchy, and dough-like texture. Traditionally crafted by boiling white yams until tender and then vigorously pounding them in a wooden mortar and pestle, this comforting delicacy is transformed into a seamless, lump-free masterpiece. Mild and subtly sweet in flavor, it serves as the ultimate canvas for rich, hearty West African soups like Egusi, Ogbono, or Efo Riro, perfectly absorbing their bold seasonings and savory juices with every bite.

Part 1: Authentic Efo Riro Recipe (Rich Spinach Stew)

Ingredients

  • 10 oz (approx. 300g) Fresh spinach (or Tatashe/Akaoko leaves), washed and roughly chopped
  • 3 large Red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 1 to 2 Scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo, adjust for spice)
  • 1 medium Red onion (half for blending, half sliced)
  • ⅓ cup Authentic palm oil
  • 2 tbsp Ground crayfish
  • 1 tbsp Iru (fermented locust beans)
  • Pre-cooked proteins: Assorted meats (beef, shaki, ponmo) and smoked fish (panla or catfish), deboned
  • 2 Bouillon cubes (crushed)
  • Salt to taste

Steps

  1. Prep the Spinach: Place the chopped spinach in a colander. Pour boiling water over it for 1 minute to wilt it slightly, then immediately rinse with cold water. Squeeze out all excess water with your hands so your stew doesn't get watery. Set aside.
  2. Blend the Peppers: Coarsely blend the red bell peppers, scotch bonnets, and half of the onion. Do not blend it smooth; it should be rough and textured. Strain out excess liquid if necessary.
  3. Bleach the Palm Oil: Heat the palm oil in a pot over medium heat for about 2 to 3 minutes (do not let it smoke excessively). Add the sliced onions and the iru (locust beans). Fry for 2 minutes until highly fragrant.
  4. Fry the Pepper Base: Add the coarsely blended peppers. Fry over medium heat for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the water evaporates and the oil begins to separate and float to the top.
  5. Add Proteins & Seasoning: Stir in your pre-cooked assorted meats, smoked fish, ground crayfish, crushed bouillon cubes, and salt. Lower the heat and let it simmer for 5 to 7 minutes so the meats absorb the rich flavors.
  6. Incorporate the Spinach: Add the tightly squeezed spinach to the pot. Stir thoroughly to combine everything. Let it simmer on low heat for an absolute maximum of 3 to 5 minutes so the spinach retains its vibrant green color. Turn off the heat.

part 2: Pounded yam recipe

ingredients

  • 1 medium tuber White Yam (specifically African Yam / Puna Yam)
  • water for boiling

Steps(Modern Food Processor Method)

Using a food processor gives you the exact same stretchy, lump-free texture as a traditional mortar and pestle without the physical strain.

  1. Prep the Yam: Peel the yam tuber and cut it into medium-sized cubes. Wash the cubes thoroughly in cold water to remove dirt and excess starch.
  2. Boil Until Fork-Tender: Place the yam cubes in a pot, cover with enough water, and add a pinch of salt if desired. Boil over medium-high heat for 20 to 25 minutes until a fork slides through the center of the cubes effortlessly. The yams must be piping hot when you blend them.
  3. Process into Swallow: Transfer the hot yam cubes directly into your food processor fitted with the regular chopping blade (do not add any water yet).
  4. Blend and Stretch: Pulse a few times to break the chunks down, then run the processor continuously for 1 to 2 minutes. You will watch the loose yam transform into a singular, stretchy, and completely smooth dough that pulls away cleanly from the sides of the bowl.
  5. Adjust Texture: If it feels too stiff, add 1 or 2 tablespoons of the hot water left over from boiling the yam and process for another 20 seconds.
  6. 1. Shape and Serve: Wet your hands or a wide spoon with a little water, scoop the pounded yam out, mold it into a smooth ball, and wrap it in plastic film or serve immediately alongside your hot Efo Riro.
